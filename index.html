What Casualty is

Three things, honestly

The raid

Two nights, no more

Six hours a week, start to finish. We expect you sharp for those six and we expect nothing from you the other 162. Heroic progression on a schedule adults can actually keep.

The keys

Groups every night

There is almost always a key going. Push groups for people chasing rating, and patient runs for people seeing a dungeon for the first time. Both are real groups, not one pretending to be the other.

The rest of it

Old raids and bad ideas

Transmog runs, achievement nights, mount farms, someone dragging eleven people into Classic at midnight. No signup, no pressure, no one keeping score.

We're probably not for you if…

  • You want a Mythic raid team. We run Heroic, we're going to finish it, and that's where this group is pointed.
  • You want loot council. We use group loot and trust people to trade.
  • You go quiet when a raid leader tells you to move. Being told to fix something isn't an insult here.
  • You only log in on raid night and want a permanent spot. The roster follows the people who show up.

What a Friday actually looks like

  1. 8:15 PM · Invites
    Get in voice. Repair, flask, food, rune. If you need a summon, say so now rather than at 8:31.
  2. 8:30 PM · First pull
    Straight into progression. The plan goes out before the raid, so nobody is hearing it for the first time.
  3. Halfway · Break
    Ten minutes. Actually ten.
  4. End of night · Last pull
    We call it on the clock, not on the boss. If something is at 4% we might steal one more, and that's the only overtime you'll ever be asked for.

What we ask of raiders

  • Roughly 80% attendance. Life happens — post in the absence channel and it's fine. Ghosting is not.
  • Show up consumed. Flask, food, weapon buff, augment rune, potions. We're not carrying your repair bill or your flask.
  • Watch the fight before we pull it. A four-minute video is cheaper than twenty wipes.
  • Sim yourself. Not for us — so you stop guessing about your own gear.
  • Be in voice. You don't have to talk. You do have to hear the call.

What you get back

  • Group loot, no council, no DKP. If it's a bigger upgrade for someone else, trade it. The one rule on top of that is trials come after the roster.
  • A real bench policy. If you're sat, you're told why, and you're not sat two weeks running.
  • Logs and VODs after every night, posted without commentary unless you ask for it.
  • Keys, alt runs and dumb old-content nights that have nothing to do with your raid spot.
  • Officers who answer. Not always instantly, but always.

Easiest way in

Apply from the Guild Finder

In game, open Guild & Communities (default hotkey J) and go to the Guild Finder tab — it's there for any character without a guild. Search Casualty, tick what you're actually after, set your availability, and send it. It drops straight into our applicant list and an officer answers within a day or two.

Or talk to a person

Whisper the guild master

/w Odyesus

Usually on in the evenings, Eastern. Honest questions get honest answers, including “you'd be happier somewhere else.” Any member can put you in front of an officer too — if you run a key with a Casualty tag, just ask them.

Once you're in

What a trial looks like

Two to three weeks raiding with us. Traded upgrades go to the established roster ahead of trials during that stretch — you'll still gear up, just not in front of someone who's been in every pull since week one. It lifts as soon as your trial does. At the end an officer gives you a straight answer and tells you what it was based on.
